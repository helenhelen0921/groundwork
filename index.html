Character data (pinyin, English definitions and structure) comes from Make Me a Hanzi by Shaunak Kishore, derived from Unicode’s Unihan database and CJKlib. It is used under the LGPL-3.0; see DATA-LICENSE.txt.

The classics are public domain. Shakespeare’s plays and sonnets and Francis Storr’s Sophocles (1912) come from Standard Ebooks (CC0). E. P. Coleridge’s Euripides (1891; Bacchae tr. T. A. Buckley, 1850) and Herbert Weir Smyth’s Aeschylus (1926) come from the Perseus Digital Library (markup CC BY-SA 3.0). Chinese titles and summaries were added for this app.

When you are online, English etymologies are looked up live from Wiktionary (text under CC BY-SA). Without a connection, the page uses its own data below.

Pronunciation, Chinese meanings, English definitions and exam tags come from ECDICT (MIT). Greek and Latin roots are adapted from Wikipedia’s List of Greek and Latin roots in English (CC BY-SA 4.0). Prefix and suffix families come from MorphyNet (CC BY-SA 3.0). roots.json and the dict folder are shared under CC BY-SA.

English word origins come from Gerard de Melo’s Etymological Wordnet (built from Wiktionary, CC BY-SA), as packaged in ety; the word list is SCOWL by Kevin Atkinson; Chinese meanings are matched from CC-CEDICT. en.json is shared under CC BY-SA.

Kangxi Dictionary (1716) text, which is in the public domain, comes from shunshi-kangxi-core (MIT). Common words come from CC-CEDICT (CC BY-SA 4.0), via cedict-json; words.json is shared under the same licence.

Shuowen Jiezi entries for other characters come from the shuowen.org data set, used under the Apache License 2.0; simplified characters were matched to traditional forms with OpenCC.

Quotations from the Shuowen Jiezi and classical texts are in the public domain. The layer stories for the excavation sites are curated by Helen. Only the Shuowen (c. AD 100) and oracle bone (c. 1200 BC) layers carry dates; the others are approximate periods.
